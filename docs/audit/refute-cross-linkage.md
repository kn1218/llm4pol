## Extra findings the audit missed (all computed)

X1. **"Copolymers" whose two monomers are the same structure.** 3,952 rows (713 polymer_ids) have `smiles1 == smiles2` byte-for-byte; 4,756 rows (757 pids) share one periodic key (adds cis/trans pairs such as P900138 polybutadiene, and polyisoprene P904685/P905211). 4,680 of the 32,994 "both matched" periodic rows (14%) are of this kind, so the cross-file "both components matched" rate over-states genuinely two-monomer linkage. Names are mostly block/graft/polycondensation entries where PoLyInfo encodes only one unit (`['block copolymer']` 861 rows). 386 rows / 27 pids even have a polymer_name string-equal to an XLSX `iupac` (e.g. `polyisoprene`, `poly(N,N-dimethylaniline)`), i.e. they are homopolymers filed in the copolymer export.

X2. **The periodic key is not a sound cut-point-invariant key when the backbone runs through a ring.** Ring-closing the two star neighbours yields the same bicyclic graph for different periodic graphs. Verified false merges: CSV 2 of 13 multi-canon keys (PPS vs PTV; 2-Me-THF vs epoxide-in-backbone); XLSX 5 of 22 (P472317/P472318, P462146/P462351, P462145/P462347, P462065/P462144, P070074/P370826); cross-file 1 (P905924). A trimer key (concatenate 3 units, then ring-close) separates all of them; the audit should re-label its "genuine cut-point variants" as 14 (XLSX) / 0 (CSV).

X3. **Encoding and record loss.** The raw file has only 51 non-ASCII bytes. It decodes as UTF-8 except 3 corrupted sequences (`EF BD 3F`, giving 9 U+FFFD with errors='replace'); reading it as cp949 mangles the UTF-8 U+FF50 of P900100 into U+7BC0+U+B993 and silently drops the following `o` (16 cells differ between the two reads). The corruption in P908185 replaced the closing `}"` of the quoted name, so 4 physical lines have an odd number of `"` and the CSV parser swallows 2 physical records: 42,559 physical records vs 42,557 parsed; sample_ids `0052076-001-002-001` and `0052078-005-001-001` are lost and polymer_id **P908186** (7,386 physical polymer_ids vs 7,385 parsed) is missing from every analysis in the audit.

X4. **Composition binding undetermined for half_aligned rows** (5,309 rows, 12.5%): mixing test 23.4 (comp<->smiles_i) vs 15.4 degC (reversed), 40 vs 69 rows - the audit's rule "resolve through the component id" has no majority-map answer for exactly one of the two ids in these rows.

X5. **Mechanical-property units.** The audit only checked tensile modulus (N 12). Same dominant-component selection gives tensile stress at break N 84 (17 pids), median ratio CSV/XLSX 0.77 (CSV median 0.0042 vs XLSX 0.0081 -> both GPa-scale); elongation N 81, ratio 0.90 (both %). Globally CSV `tensile_modulus` p50 0.255 vs XLSX 2.1 GPa - the CSV copolymer set is dominated by elastomers, so a global comparison is not a unit test.

X6. **Both-composition >= 95 rows** (70 rows, 46 block / 18 graft) carry values like `w133/w114`, `w750/w2420`, `m113/m113`: compositions there are not percentages (likely block lengths / DP), and 21 of them are inside the C6 Tg comparison.

X7. **XLSX duplicates are not clean.** 91 linear-canon groups (192 pids) have >1 pid; 64 of them have different iupac strings (spelling variants, E/Z in name only, e.g. P330008 vs P190002 `poly(dimethylketene)`). Where >=2 Tg values exist (33 groups) the within-group Tg range has median 6.5 K but 4 groups differ by >50 K; under the periodic key 43 groups, 6 with >50 K spread. Taking the median across pids (as the audit does) hides these.

X8. **CSV within-polymer Tg scatter**: 2,667 polymer_ids have >=2 Tg samples; median within-pid range 20.2 degC, 588 pids exceed 50 degC. The 15 degC residual in C6 is therefore at the level of the CSV's own sample scatter, supporting the unit conclusion.

X9. 13,350 rows (2,735 pids) have both monomers matched to XLSX homopolymers AND a Tg value; 6,917 rows (1,381 pids) additionally have both compositions parseable - the usable core for any mixing-rule / copolymer-Tg modelling.
