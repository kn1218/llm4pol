# Adversarial citation check — POLYMER-LANDSCAPE.md (REF-101 … REF-153)

Date: 2026-09-11. Target file: `scratchpad/landscape/POLYMER-LANDSCAPE.md`.

Method: every claim's primary URL opened with WebFetch; where the publisher returned 403/303 (ACS, Nature, RSC, MDPI, ScienceDirect, PubMed, ChemRxiv), the same URL was re-opened through the Exa fetch tool, which returns publisher text; residual gaps were closed with targeted WebSearch on exact phrases. "Confirmed" below means title + first author + year + venue matched AND the headline stated fact was found in the source. Numeric sub-facts that could not be located are listed explicitly as caveats — they are *not* silently treated as verified.

**Score: 50 confirmed, 2 refuted (REF-112, REF-140), 1 unverifiable (REF-141).** No fabricated reference was found: every one of the 53 URLs points at a real, correctly-titled work except REF-141's Authorea preprint (unreachable and not indexed under the stated title) and REF-140's Berkeley PDF mirror (connection refused). The landscape's overall reliability is high; the defects are attribution errors and over-precise paraphrases, not invented sources.

## 1. The three findings that must be fixed

### REF-112 — REFUTED (wrong first author, and the landscape's author list is partly invented)

Polymer **218**:123495 (2021), "Predicting glass transition of amorphous polymers by application of cheminformatics and molecular dynamics simulations" is real and the science claim is right (QSPR over ~100 polymers; features grouped into cohesive energy, chain stiffness, grafting density; CG-MD used for mechanistic interpretation, not quantitative prediction — all four Highlights confirmed on ScienceDirect).

But the author list is **Anas Karuth, Amirhadi Alesadi, Wenjie Xia, Bakhtiyor Rasulev**. The claim's "Alesadi/Xia et al. 2021" names a second author as first author, and the landscape body (line 60) writes "**Alesadi, Cao, Li, Xia et al.**" — *Cao* and *Li* are not authors of this paper at all. Fix to `Karuth, Alesadi, Xia, Rasulev (2021)`.

Also unverified: the stated Tg span "145–482 K". What is verifiable from citing sources is an SVR test-set R² 0.937 / RMSE 25.0 K on 79 polymers.

### REF-140 — REFUTED (dead URL + inverted mechanism)

`http://alchemy.cchem.berkeley.edu/static/pdf/papers/2025JAmChemSocEPezoulas.pdf` → **ECONNREFUSED**. The paper itself is real: Ethan R. Pezoulas … Jeffrey R. Long, *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* **2025**, 147(25), 21832–21843, doi `10.1021/jacs.5c04689` (open copy: PMC12203610). Replace the URL.

The mechanism is stated backwards. The paper reports that **electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions drive short-chain PFAS adsorption, while hydrophobic and fluorophilic interactions improve long-chain PFAS adsorption**. The claim's "combining electrostatic with fluorophilic interactions … outperforms commercial anion-exchange resins **on short-chain PFAS**" mis-assigns fluorophilicity to the short-chain regime. Two further conflations: "<30 s" is the *equilibration time* for PPN-6-FNDMB (capacity up to 4.0 mmol/g), and "near-complete removal" refers to **21 different PFAS in a groundwater sample from a US Air Force base**, not specifically to short-chain species in deionized water.

### REF-141 — UNVERIFIABLE (Authorea unreachable; title does not match the published record)

`https://doi.org/10.22541/au.176497757.75053953/v1` resolves to authorea.com, which returned 403 on WebFetch and timed out twice on Exa. No record is indexed under the claimed title "Fluorine-free, Chemically Modular Polymer Adsorbents…". The work almost certainly exists as the preprint of **Dhamania & Moon, "Chemically Modular, Nonfluorinated Polymer Adsorbents for Capturing Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS)", *Energy & Environmental Materials* (2026), doi `10.1002/eem2.70435`** — active-ester click chemistry, modular non-fluorinated gel library, both confirmed by the journal page and the UF press release. **Cite the journal version.** The quantitative claims (>98 % PFOA removal, 910 mg/g, piperazine/piperidine ligands, PEG-diacrylate networks, fixed-charge-density comparison) remain unchecked against any accessible source.

## 4. Items the landscape missed (2025–2026), ranked by how much they change the plan

### Oracles and benchmarks — the biggest gap

1. **SimPoly + the PolyArena benchmark** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13696 (Simm, Hélie, Schulz … Sun; Oct 2025). A **machine-learning force field that predicts polymer density and Tg from first principles without fitting to experimental data**, plus "a benchmark of experimental bulk properties for **130 polymers** and an accompanying quantum-chemical dataset". This is the single most important omission: it is a candidate middle tier between the millisecond surrogate and the 10³–10⁴ core-hour all-atom MD of §1.6, and PolyArena is a ready-made standardised Tg/density evaluation that §6's metric set should adopt. PolyJarvis (REF-102) already cites it as ref 42.
2. **OPoly26 — The Open Polymers 2026 Dataset and Evaluations** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23117 (Levine, Liesen … Blau, Antoniuk; Dec 2025, rev. Mar 2026). **6.57 million DFT calculations on ≤360-atom polymer-derived clusters, >1.2 billion atoms**, varying monomer composition, **chain length, chain architecture and solvation**. Open and redistributable (unlike PoLyInfo) and architecture-aware — directly relevant to §3.2 and to any MLFF-based oracle.
3. **Discovery of Polymer Electrolytes with Bayesian Optimization and High-Throughput MD** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17595 (2026). A second MD-in-the-loop BO system besides SPACIER; §1.1 and §4 currently imply SPACIER is the only one.
4. **Scaling Law of Sim2Real Transfer Learning in Expanding Computational Materials Databases** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04042 (Yoshida group, RadonPy lineage). Quantifies how much simulated data is needed to calibrate to experiment — exactly the +40–120 K MD-offset problem of §1.4, which the landscape treats only as a constant or linear correction.
5. **Omics-scale polymer computational database transferable to real-world AI applications** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11626 (RadonPy lineage, 2025). The successor data resource to REF-101's 1,001-polymer validation set.

### Builders and conformers (§1.3 is incomplete)

6. **Polyply** — https://github.com/marrink-lab/polyply_1.0 — automated polymer topology and coordinate generation; cited by PolyJarvis (ref 18) as one of two tools automating specific MD stages. Absent from §1.3.
7. **PolyConf: Unlocking Polymer Conformation Generation through Hierarchical Generative Models** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08859 (2025). Direct competitor to polyGen (REF-108) for fast 3D conformer generation.
8. **Atomistic Polymer Modeling: Recent Advances and Challenges in Building and Parametrization Workflows** — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.macromol.5c01166 (*Macromolecules* 2025). A 2025 review covering precisely the cell-builder / force-field-typing landscape of §1.3; lets §1.3 cite one survey instead of five repos.

### Design space and synthesizability (§3)

9. **polyVERSE** — https://github.com/Ramprasad-Group/polyVERSE (Zenodo 13352644).
