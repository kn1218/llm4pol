## 0. Executive summary (what changes the porting plan)

1. **There is no cheap GCMC-equivalent oracle for Tg.** In LLM4MOF each iteration evaluates up to 10 candidates per beam × 4 beams with RASPA3 GCMC after PORMAKE assembly, LAMMPS/UFF relaxation and Zeo++ (Manuscript lines 233-236; SI Note S1). The polymer analogue of "one GCMC run" is an all-atom MD Tg protocol that costs roughly **10^3–10^4 CPU-hours per candidate** (Section 1.6 cost table; RadonPy itself reports 30–50 h of wall time on a 40-core dual-Xeon workstation just for equilibration, REF-101) and carries a **systematic +40 to +120 K cooling-rate offset** versus experiment (Afzal 2021: mean +79.1 K, calibrated MAE 27.5 K, REF-104; Martí 2024: constant offset 105 K, R² 0.83, REF-105; Gudla & Zhang 2024: "overestimation averaging between 80 and 120 K", REF-106; PolyJarvis 2026: +38 to +47 K on PE/aPS/PEG, REF-102). MD is therefore a **top-k validator** (as in Zhou 2026, 8 candidates, REF-124; Bhati 2026, 50 candidates, prior-art REF-27), not an inner-loop oracle. This confirms the pre-proposal's "two-tier oracle" but shows that the tier-2 budget is ~1 candidate/day on a few hundred CPU cores.
2. **The inner-loop oracle must be a fast surrogate, and the interpretable baseline should be group contribution + Fox.** Van Krevelen / Bicerano group contributions (REF-113/114) give in-sample Tg MAE ≈ 10 K on curated sets (Weyland, quoted by Brierley-Croft 2025, REF-115) but do not transfer to motifs outside the table; Roy 2026 measured group-contribution R² 0.71 vs single-LLM 0.67 vs their agent 0.78 on a 50-polymer Tg benchmark (REF-125). For copolymers the Fox equation reaches R² 0.835 / RMSE 42.6 °C on PoLyInfo binary copolymers (prior-art REF-26) versus 0.92 / 24.5 °C for the best GNN — a mandatory baseline. ML oracles with public weights: polyBERT (HF, academic licence), PolyFusionAgent (HF + GitHub), PolyNC (Apache-2.0), TransPolymer (MIT) — see prior-art REF-9/16/11/10 and citation-check items 2/11. Polymer Genome is a web service only (prior-art REF-38).
3. **The PORMAKE analogue exists and is rule-based virtual forward synthesis.** Three public generators enumerate synthesizable polymers from purchasable monomers with reaction templates: **OMG** (17 polymerization reactions × 77,281 eMolecules reactants → ~12 M CRUs, GPL-3.0 code, Zenodo data, REF-116), **SMiPoly** (22 rules: 6 chain-growth + 16 step-growth; 1,083 monomers → 169,347 polymers in 7 classes; BSD-3, REF-117), and Ramprasad's **VFS / pvfsga** (7 M ROP polymers; scaffold + R-group GA over SMARTS reaction templates, REF-118/119). The LLM4MOF "(topology, node, linker) → constraint JSON → Matchmaker" maps naturally onto "(polymerization class, monomer A, monomer B, composition, architecture) → constraint JSON → Matchmaker" over such a library, with the project's own 4,792 CSV monomers + 13,661 XLSX repeat units as the "database mode" pool (DATA-FOUNDATION-REPORT §1.4/1.8).
4. **Synthesizability filters are immature for polymers.** All 2025-2026 agents (Polymer-Agent, polyBART, PolyTAO, POLYT5) use the small-molecule SA score (Ertl) or SCScore on the monomer/repeat unit (REF-120/121/127/128); Trepalin 2026 tests SA score as a filter for virtual polymers (REF-122); polyRETRO (2026) predicts polymerization class + monomers from a target SMILES with an LLM (REF-123). None is a validated polymer synthesizability oracle; reaction-template membership (OMG/SMiPoly) is currently the strongest guarantee.
5. **Diagnostic beams / causal attribution have no polymer precedent.** Of the 2024-2026 polymer agents surveyed (Polymer-Agent, PolyFusionAgent, PolySea, PolyJarvis, Roy 2026, Zhou 2026, POLYT5-agent), none isolates *which design axis* (backbone vs side group vs composition vs architecture) produced a gain via controlled comparison arms; interpretability is post-hoc SHAP/attention/Integrated-Gradients (REF-131..136). The closest structural precedents are (a) CI-LLM/HAPPY, which tokenises repeat units into chemically meaningful subgroups and attributes predictions to them (REF-136), and (b) PolyBench-LLM's SME-curated descriptor vocabulary (prior-art REF-21). The LLM4MOF controlled vocabulary (SI Table S4: 124 canonical tags + 215-entry alias map, emitted only by Agent 2) has **no published polymer counterpart**; the PoLyInfo ontology (prior-art REF-7, CC BY 4.0) is the nearest reusable tag source.
6. **Evaluation practice.** Generative-polymer papers report validity / uniqueness / novelty / Tanimoto similarity / SA score (PolyTAO, REF-127; Yue 2025 MOSES metrics, REF-129) and, increasingly, **one synthesized polymer** (polyBART Td, POLYT5, Kern 2025 thiocane, Zheng 2024 vitrimer, SPACIER optical polymers; REF-128/118/130/103) or **MD validation of top-k** (Zhou 2026, Bhati 2026). LLM4MOF's own editor response states "We have not carried out experimental validation" and instead releases building blocks, force fields, protocols and reasoning traces (ResponseToEditor lines 71-75). For LLM4POL, MD validation of top-k (cheap enough for ~10 finalists) is the realistic minimum; hit-rate-vs-database-top-1% and enrichment factor (LLM4MOF Table S2) transfer directly to database mode on the PoLyInfo-derived pool.
7. **PFAS (Phase 2) has no public polymer-adsorbent dataset.** The best-structured ML sets are 457 literature points on 21 PFAS × 16 polyamide NF/RO membranes (Jeong et al. 2024, REF-138) and a resin-screening set for 43 PFASs (Zhang et al. 2025, REF-139); Dangayach 2025 is a *review* of ML inverse design for membranes, not a PFAS dataset (REF-137). Adsorbent papers (Pezoulas 2025 PPNs, Dhamania 2025 fluorine-free networks, Hassan 2025 mixtures) each report tens of materials (REF-140..142). A ~200-500 polymer PFAS set, as the pre-proposal assumes, would have to be literature-mined and would be heterogeneous in conditions (pH, matrix, PFAS species).

## 1. SIMULATION ORACLES (the GCMC analogue)

### 1.1 RadonPy
- **REF-101** Hayashi, Shiomi, Morikawa, Yoshida (2022) "RadonPy: automated physical property calculation using all-atom classical molecular dynamics simulations for polymer informatics", npj Comput. Mater. 8:222. URLs: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-022-00906-4 (search-result-only; Exa excerpts), https://github.com/RadonPy/RadonPy (**fetched**), https://github.com/RadonPy/RadonPy/blob/develop/AutoMD_scripts/README.md (search-result-only). verified_by: **fetched** (GitHub) + search-result-only (paper text).
  - Properties (paper, first release): 15 amorphous-state properties from equilibrium MD (density, Rg, Cp, Cv, compressibilities, bulk moduli, expansion coefficients, self-diffusion, refractive index, static dielectric constant, nematic order parameter) + NEMD thermal conductivity/diffusivity. **Tg was not in the 2022 release** (confirmed by Martí 2024: "Tg is a notable absent from the list of RadonPy properties", REF-105).
  - Current GitHub README: "the library comprises the calculation of 62 properties"; the feature list now includes **"Glass transition temperature (Tg)"**, a preset `sim/preset/tg.py`, an `AutoMD_scripts/4_tg.py` ("Calculation of Tg by NEMD simulation using the preset sim.preset.tg") with output columns `tg`, `tg_cooling_rate`, `tg_interval_temp`, `tg_rmse`; also solubility parameters, cohesive energy density, dynamic dielectric properties, Abbe number; chain builders for homopolymer, alternating/random/block copolymers, branched polymers; cells for amorphous, mixtures, solutions, crystalline. Copolymer ratio/type are environment variables in `1_eq.py` (`RadonPy_Copoly_Ratio`, `RadonPy_Copoly_Type`).
  - Dependencies: Python 3.9-3.13, **LAMMPS ≥ 3Mar20**, RDKit, **Psi4 ≥ 1.5** (RESP charges; not pip-installable), resp, dftd3, mdtraj. Default force field GAFF2_mod; defaults NAtom 1000/chain × NChain 10 = ~10,000 atoms.
  - **Licence: BSD 3-Clause** (GitHub metadata).
  - Cost statement (paper): "an equilibrium MD simulation of a conventional amorphous polymer requires an average run time of more than 30–50 h based on our experiments conducted on a workstation with a dual CPU (Intel Xeon Gold 6148; 2.4 GHz) having 40 cores" → ~1,200–2,000 core-hours per equilibration; ">1000 unique amorphous polymers were computed in ~2 months mainly using the supercomputer Fugaku", five independent replicates each. Protocol: 21-step Larsen compression/decompression (~1.5 ns) + NPT ≥5 ns checked every 5 ns, failure after 50 ns; 1 fs step, SHAKE, PPPM.
  - Accuracy vs PoLyInfo (1,001 polymers): density R² 0.890 (slope 0.805); refractive index, density, thermal conductivity "reproduced the experimental values quantitatively"; Cp correlated but biased (classical); expansion coefficients weakly correlated; ML transfer-learning calibration cut MAE by 69 %/87 % for the two expansion coefficients.
- **REF-103** Nanjo, Arifin, Maeda, Hayashi, Hatakeyama-Sato, Himeno, Hayakawa, Yoshida (2025) "SPACIER: on-demand polymer design with fully automated all-atom classical molecular dynamics integrated into machine learning pipelines", npj Comput. Mater. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-024-01492-3 — verified_by: **fetched**. Open-source BO loop wrapping RadonPy; proof-of-concept **synthesized optical polymers beyond the refractive-index/Abbe-number Pareto front**. This is the closest existing "MD-in-the-loop polymer design" system and the natural code reference for an LLM4POL discovery mode.

### 1.2 PolyJarvis (LLM + RadonPy/EMC + LAMMPS)
- **REF-102** Zhao, Chandrasekhar, Barati Farimani (2026) "PolyJarvis: An LLM-Orchestrated Agent for Automated All-Atom Molecular Dynamics of Amorphous Homopolymers", arXiv:2604.02537 (v1 2026-04-02 titled "...Autonomous Large Language Model Agent..."; later version). URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02537 (**fetched**), https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02537v1 (search-result-only), https://github.com/arz-2/PolyJarvis (search-result-only). verified_by: **fetched**.
  - Architecture: stateful Claude orchestrator + 13 stateless specialist workers over three MCP servers (EMC amorphous-cell builder with PCFF/OPLS-AA/TraPPE-UA for 20 polymer classes; LAMMPS engine; RadonPy v0.2.10 as fallback builder). v1 used RadonPy only + Lambda Labs 4× Quadro RTX 6000 GPUs.
  - Validation: nine homopolymers (PE, PS, PMMA, PEG, PEEK, PVC, PLA, PSU, cis-PBD); acceptance criteria **Tg within 50 K, density within 5 %, bulk modulus within 30 %**; 18/25 comparisons pass (Tg 7/9, density 5/9, K 6/7). v1: PMMA Tg 395 K within +10–18 K, the other three polymers overestimate Tg by **+38 to +47 K**, "attributable primarily to the intrinsic MD cooling-rate bias rather than agent error". Cells 3,600–10,900 atoms, 8–10 chains, GPU LAMMPS; Tg from stepwise cooling + bilinear fit.
  - Relevance: shows an LLM can drive the MD oracle, but the authors state the accuracy "is suited to screening ... does not replace production simulations"; homopolymers only, no copolymers yet.

### 1.4 MD Tg protocols and the cooling-rate offset
- **REF-104** Afzal, Browning, Goldberg, Halls, Gavartin, Morisato, Hughes, Giesen, Goose (2021) "High-Throughput Molecular Dynamics Simulations and Validation of Thermophysical Properties of Polymers for Various Applications", ACS Appl. Polym. Mater. 3:620 (CC-BY-NC-ND). https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsapm.0c00524 (**fetched**, header/abstract), full text via ChemRxiv https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.12250229.v2 (search-result-only). verified_by: **fetched** (page) + search-result-only (numbers).
  - 315 polymers, 10 replicas each, GPU Desmond/OPLS; hyperbola fit of density–T. Density vs Bicerano experimental: R² 0.95, MAE 3.5 %. **Raw MD Tg overestimates experiment by +79.1 K on average; linear fit slope 1.30; calibrated Tg = 0.77·Tg(calc) + 21.08 K gives MAE 27.5 K (7.98 %), RMSE 36.2 K**; polyimides MAE 45.5 K, polyamides 32.0 K after calibration; WLF argument: ≈3 K per decade of rate.
- **REF-105** Martí, Pétuya, Bosoni, Dublanchet, Mohr, Léonforte (2024) "Predicting the Glass Transition Temperature of Biopolymers via High-Throughput Molecular Dynamics Simulations and Machine Learning", ACS Appl. Polym. Mater. 6:4449. https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsapm.3c03040 (**fetched**); preprint https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2023-t1c4n (search-result-only). 58 homopolymers + 488 copolymers, 4 replicas, **2,184 simulations, 143.052 µs total (~65 ns per simulation)**; 47 homopolymers vs experiment: R² 0.83 with a **constant +105.02 K offset**; ML surrogate on the 546 MD Tg values MAE 19.3 K; "it would have taken node-years to simulate them".
- **REF-106** Gudla & Zhang (2024) "How to Determine Glass Transition Temperature of Polymer Electrolytes from Molecular Dynamics Simulations", J. Phys. Chem. B 128:10537. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11533182/ — verified_by: **fetched**. "The computed values of Tg in polymer systems derived from MD simulations tend to exhibit an overestimation averaging between 80 and 120 K"; cooling rates 5000→5 K/ns shift Tg by ≈30 K; 5 vs 40 K/ns differ by 10–20 K (Klajmon); recommends slope–temperature analysis and reporting T/Tg.
- **REF-110** Suter, Müller, Vassaux, Anastasiou, Simmons, Tilbrook, Coveney (2025) "Rapid, Accurate and Reproducible Prediction of the Glass Transition Temperature Using Ensemble-Based Molecular Dynamics Simulation", JCTC 21:1405. https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acs.jctc.4c01364 — verified_by: **fetched**. Single-replica 95 % CI ≈ 60–80 K; **≥10 replicas needed for CI < 20 K**; concurrent-temperature protocol (4 ns burn-in + 2 ns production per temperature) cuts wall-clock from days to hours; for cross-linked epoxies no cooling-rate correction was needed.
- **REF-111** Odegard group (2022) "Accurate predictions of thermoset resin glass transition temperatures from all-atom molecular dynamics simulation", Soft Matter. https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2022/sm/d2sm00851c — verified_by: search-result-only. Accurate Tg without rate correction **only if model density matches experiment within 2 %**.
- Additional protocol references (search-result-only): Polymer J. 2020 PEO benchmark (cooling rate ≤5×10¹³ K/min, ≥11,240 g/mol, ≥10 chains) https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41428-020-00443-1 ; Bhati 2026 protocol (prior-art REF-27): 6 chains × ~2,000 atoms, 3 replicates, 900→100 K in 10 K steps × 5 ns each (= 400 ns per replicate) on GPU Desmond.
- **Take-away:** an honest MD Tg oracle needs 3–10 replicates × 100–400 ns each, and its absolute values must be **calibrated** (linear map or constant offset of +40–120 K) before comparison with PoLyInfo; rank-ordering is reliable (R² 0.83–0.92), absolute values are not.

### 1.6 Realistic cost table (CPU-hours per candidate)

Basis: LAMMPS single-core throughput ≈ 220–230 katom-step/s for a 32,000-atom CHARMM + PPPM system (REF-143, LAMMPS docs, **fetched**), i.e. ~22 timesteps/s for a 10,000-atom class-I cell → **≈1.9 ns/day per core at 1 fs**, ≈12.6 core-h per ns at 100 % efficiency; take **15–18 core-h/ns** for realistic MPI efficiency on ~32 cores (LAMMPS docs: >50 % efficiency needs a few hundred atoms/core, REF-143). RadonPy's empirical anchor (30–50 h × 40 cores ≈ 1,200–2,000 core-h per equilibration, REF-101) is consistent with ~70–130 ns of total equilibration + DFT/RESP overhead. Implications for the project's hardware:
- **dirac (CPU PBS, LAMMPS available per hpc-submit skill; GPU unknown):** at ~4,000–20,000 CPU-h per Tg candidate, a 256-core allocation delivers **≈0.3–1.5 Tg candidates per day**. LLM4MOF's discovery mode evaluates up to 40 structures per iteration (10 per beam × 4 beams) for 10 iterations × 5 replicates = up to 2,000 GCMC runs per task; the same design with MD Tg would need 10⁷–10⁸ CPU-h. Hence: database mode on the PoLyInfo-derived pool (free), surrogate oracle in the loop, MD only for ≤10 finalists per task (≈10⁵ CPU-h, i.e. a few weeks on dirac).
- **RTX 5050 8 GB (no CUDA torch, no LAMMPS GPU build recorded):** an 8–10 k-atom class-I cell fits easily in 8 GB; GPU LAMMPS (KOKKOS/GPU package) is what PolyJarvis uses "predominantly on a single GPU per run" (REF-102) with 24 GB Quadros. Throughput on the 5050 is **not measured**; expect tens of ns/day (unverified), i.e. ~1 Tg replicate per week — acceptable for spot validation, not for a loop. Psi4 RESP charges are CPU-bound anyway.
- **Density-only oracle** is the one MD quantity that is both cheap (≈1 day on 32 cores) and quantitatively validated (REF-101/104); it is the polymer analogue of "Zeo++ geometry" and could gate candidates before a Tg run, mirroring MOF2Zeo → Zeo++ → GCMC.

### 2.1 Group-contribution (GC) methods — interpretable baselines
- **REF-113** van Krevelen & te Nijenhuis (2009) *Properties of Polymers*, 4th ed., Elsevier, ISBN 978-0-08-054819-7. https://shop.elsevier.com/books/properties-of-polymers/van-krevelen/978-0-08-054819-7 — verified_by: **fetched**. Subtitle "Their Numerical Estimation and Prediction from Additive Group Contributions"; Part II "Transition temperatures"; Part VII "Survey of group contributions in additive molar quantities" (Tg via molar glass-transition function Yg = Σ contributions / M). Group tables for Tg (e.g., –CH2– 158, p-phenylene 2820, –SO2– 2150, –NHCO– 1510 per the transcribed table in REF-146) are reproduced in the Kaggle 2025 write-up (REF-146, **fetched**).
- **REF-114** Bicerano (2002) *Prediction of Polymer Properties*, 3rd ed., CRC/Marcel Dekker, 784 pp. https://www.routledge.com/Prediction-of-Polymer-Properties/Bicerano/p/book/9780824708214 — verified_by: search-result-only (WebFetch 403). Connectivity-index (Randić χ) based QSPR for Tg, density etc.; Bicerano's tables are the experimental reference set used by Afzal 2021 (315 polymers) and Bhati 2026 (315 homopolymers).
- **REF-115** Brierley-Croft, Olmsted, Hine, Mandle, Chaplin, Grasmeder, Mattsson (2025) "Polymer Informatics Method for Fast and Accurate Prediction of the Glass Transition Temperature from Chemical Structure", Macromolecules 58:6407 (CC-BY); arXiv:2411.06461. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06461 — verified_by: **fetched**. ### 2.2 Copolymer mixing rules
- **REF-147** Brostow, Chiu, Kalogeras, Vassilikou-Dova (2008) "Prediction of glass transition temperatures: Binary blends and copolymers", Mater. Lett. 62:3152. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167577X08001870 — verified_by: **fetched** (page; equations from search excerpt). Fox: 1/Tg = w1/Tg1 + w2/Tg2 (Fox 1956, Bull. Am. Phys. Soc. 1:123); Gordon–Taylor: Tg = (w1·Tg1 + K·w2·Tg2)/(w1 + K·w2) with one fitted K; Kwei adds an interaction term; the paper proposes a cubic-deviation equation. 341 citations.
- **REF-148** Huang, Du, Zhang, Liu (2022) "Glass Transition Temperatures of Copolymers: Molecular Origins of Deviation from the Linear Relation", Macromolecules 55:3189. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.macromol.1c02287 — verified_by: **fetched**. Negative deviation ↔ mono-substituted + asymmetric di-substituted monomer pairs; positive deviation ↔ mono-substituted + symmetric di-substituted pairs; a **mechanistic rule set usable as LLM4POL vocabulary tags** (M-type/AD-type/SD-type monomer).
- **REF-149** Branham-Ferrari & Simmons (2026) "The Interplay Between Liquid-Liquid Phase Equilibria, Sequence, and Tg in Copolymers", arXiv:2602.18659. https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2602.18659 — verified_by: search-result-only. MD shows Fox deviations are set by exchange energy sign (UCST-like → negative, LCST-like → positive) and grow with sequence alternation; Taylor, Priestley, Register (2025) J. Phys. Chem. B https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.5c03909 (search-result-only; ACS timeout) measured random S/I copolymers ≈3 °C below Fox, −13 °C after hydrogenation. These justify keeping `copolymer_type` (alternating/random/block) as a design axis.
- Baseline numbers on PoLyInfo copolymers: Fox R² 0.835 / RMSE 42.57 °C vs WCS-GCN 0.921 / 24.45 °C (prior-art REF-26); Kuenneth 2021 composition-weighted fingerprints Tg RMSE 20.5 K (prior-art REF-24); the data audit's own linear-mixing test (DATA-FOUNDATION-REPORT §1.3) gives median |ΔTg| ≈ 14 °C when composition is bound to the correct component.

## 6. EVALUATION PRACTICE FOR POLYMER INVERSE DESIGN

What the 2024-2026 papers report (all URLs above):
1. **Generation quality:** validity (RDKit parse + exactly two `*`), uniqueness@k, novelty vs training set, Tanimoto similarity/diversity, FCD, SA score — PolyTAO (REF-127), Yue 2025 (REF-129; MOSES metrics adapted with the two-star rule), polyBART (REF-128), POLYT5.
2. **Property targeting:** fraction of candidates whose *predicted* property crosses a threshold (Tg > 500/600 K; Kim 2021 REF-151; polyBART REF-128; POLYT5), or shift of the predicted distribution under RL (Yue 2025).
3. **Physics validation of top-k:** MD on 8 designed copolymers (Zhou 2026, REF-124, 4.17 % mean relative error), MD on 50 grid points (Bhati 2026), MD-calibrated GP for 8,424 vitrimers (Zheng 2024, REF-130), DFT band gap for the POLYT5 candidate.
4. **Experimental validation of ≥1 candidate:** polyBART (Td), POLYT5 (dielectric polymer), Kern 2025 (thiocane, ROP), Zheng 2024 (vitrimer Tg 311–317 K), SPACIER (optical polymers), liquid-crystalline polyimides (npj Comput. Mater. 2025, http://www.npg.nature.com/articles/s41524-025-01671-w.pdf, search-result-only). Every 2025-2026 *Nature-portfolio* polymer inverse-design paper found here includes at least one synthesized material; arXiv/JCIM agent papers (Polymer-Agent, PolyFusionAgent, Roy 2026) do not.
5. **Baselines used:** random sampling, GA, BO (LLM4MOF Fig. 6/7 uses exactly these three plus a generative model; Manuscript lines 317-362); Fox equation and GC for copolymer/homopolymer Tg (prior-art REF-26; REF-125); ChemCrow and single-LLM prompting (REF-125).
6. **Splits/leakage:** random or k-fold splits dominate (prior-art REF-47); Kaggle OPC documented a Tg shift 102.9→179.8 °C that broke leaderboard models (prior-art REF-34); PolyTrace uses scaffold-grouped CV (citation-check item 5).
7. **Whether reviewers demanded validation:** the only reviewer correspondence available is LLM4MOF's own (ResponseToEditor lines 71-75): the authors declined experimental validation and instead released building blocks, force fields, simulation protocols and reasoning traces; the editor's three requests were answered with "new experiments and new analysis" including label-demand measurements for generative baselines (lines 6, 39-50). No polymer paper's review file was accessible, so "required to add validation" cannot be verified; the observable pattern is that journal versions (npj, Nat. Commun., Chem. Sci.) carry experimental or MD validation and preprints/agent papers often do not.

**Recommended metric set for LLM4POL (derived):** hit rate vs PoLyInfo top-1 %/top-10 % thresholds and enrichment factor per beam (LLM4MOF Table S2 style, pooled over replicates, Mann–Whitney vs random beam); novelty vs PoLyInfo (canonical repeat-unit key, DATA-FOUNDATION-REPORT §3.2); validity (two-star rule); synthesizability by template membership + SA score reported; diversity (IntDiv); calibrated-MD Tg on ≤10 finalists with ≥3 replicates and the +offset stated; Fox/GC/GA/BO/random baselines under the same oracle.
