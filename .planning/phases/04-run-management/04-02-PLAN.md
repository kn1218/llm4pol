**One task, twelve files, and why it is not split.** The end-to-end test passes only when the
selector, the populations, the driver, the reducer and the entry point all exist: a `run` verb
that records a selection without its evaluation events, or a `run_opened` without its populations,
would write a record in a shape no later plan keeps, and a closed run without `results.csv` would
leave `replay` nothing to reproduce. Seams that were looked for and rejected: `run` without
`reduce` (above); `meta.json` without its schema (D-39: the writer validates before it writes);
the plan selector without its schema (caller-supplied JSON would enter unchecked). What was split
off and lives elsewhere: everything below the entry point (plan 04-01) and the committed examples
with their inventory entries (plan 04-04).

Kept out of this plan so the tracer stays thin, each a functionality gap that a named later plan
fills without changing the shape: `resume`, state rebuilt from the ledger, refusals, exit 3 (04-05);
the redaction rule, `dirty` over the behaviour paths, the two populations shown apart (04-06);
`usage.json` and the `usage` verb (04-07); `run_summary.json` and the pinned fixture (04-08).

Definitions, each taken from the ADR table: `n_selected` is the length of the recorded selection (0 for `no_match`); `n_ok` counts selected candidates whose objective result has status `ok`, cached or not; `median_objective` is `statistics.median` of those values, no value when `n_ok` is 0; a selected candidate is feasible when every constraint result is `ok` and satisfies its operator against the threshold of the problem spec; `feasible_frac` is the feasible count over `n_selected`, no value when `n_selected` is 0; `n_population` counts members with a non-null objective; `pct_of_population` is `100 * n_worse / n_population` computed as an integer product followed by one division, where worse is strictly below the median for direction `max` and strictly above it for `min`, no value when there is no median; a member is feasible when its objective and every constraint value are non-null and every constraint holds, m is their count, the two threshold indices are `(m + 9) // 10` and `(m + 99) // 100` in integer arithmetic, the threshold is the objective of the member at that index counted from the best, and a hit is a feasible selected candidate whose objective is at least as good as the threshold; with m equal to 0 the two hit cells carry no value. Objective key, direction, operators and thresholds come from `ledger.problem`. `render_csv` writes into `io.StringIO(newline="")` with `csv.writer(..., lineterminator="\n")`, floats as `repr`, integers as `str`, no value as the empty field, and returns UTF-8 bytes without a byte order mark (Code Example 4, F-27, F-29). `write_outputs(run_dir)` reads the ledger, writes `results.csv` with an exclusive binary open and returns the sha256 by file name.
